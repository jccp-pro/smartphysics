At 100% retention, the Fourier reconstruction is mathematically equivalent to the original decoded samples (up to numerical rounding). At lower percentages, omitted frequencies introduce an approximation. The two halves always add together to produce the retained-component reconstruction, before output quantization.

Physics and signal-integrity notes

How to interpret the experiment accurately.

ℹ

Fourier principle. Any finite digitised audio segment can be represented by sine/cosine components of different amplitudes, frequencies and phases. The app performs a discrete Fourier transform (FFT) and synthesises audio by an inverse FFT (IFFT). The constant (DC) and Nyquist components are included as special Fourier terms.

Two complementary halves. The selected decoded channel (left/right/mix) is the analysis input; the imported encoded file remains unchanged. Half A contains one subset of retained frequencies and Half B contains the rest. Half A + Half B = reconstructed signal (within numerical precision). The split is by number of frequency components, not equal duration, volume or energy. Individual halves may sound very different and may be much quieter.

Long recordings. For efficiency, automatic mode analyses long selections in successive independent FFT blocks. These are still genuine Fourier decompositions, but of individual blocks rather than a single global spectrum; filtering out components may introduce block-edge artifacts. Choose a short interval and “One FFT” for a global decomposition of that interval.

IR electronics. A 38–40 kHz carrier is only recoverable from audio whose sampling rate and acquisition chain preserve it. If the original phone recording was sampled at 48 kHz, an actual 40 kHz optical carrier cannot have been captured directly; do not confuse inferred timing or synthesized carriers with measured ones. Ordinary phone speakers, headphone outputs, and Bluetooth audio paths cannot be assumed to transmit a 40 kHz electrical waveform faithfully.

Channel layout matters. If an IR LED is wired across the left and right headphone contacts, it is driven by L−R. Stereo IR recordings often put opposite-polarity pulses on L and R for this reason. A mono track sends the same signal to both sides, so L−R = 0 and the LED receives nothing, even though the waveform plot looks correct. Keep the output layout on Original stereo or Differential for that wiring.

Privacy and file formats. No audio is uploaded to a server. Generated WAV downloads contain 16-bit PCM in the chosen output layout (mono, original stereo, or differential) and can clip signals outside ±1; any clipping is reported. The original imported file can also be downloaded byte-for-byte. AAC downloads are lossy compressed (ADTS .aac using WebCodecs, or AAC-in-MP4 .m4a via the native recorder where supported). AAC may change high-frequency content and timing, particularly at 38–40 kHz. The app never disguises another audio format as AAC. Browser AAC support varies; if unsupported, use WAV.
