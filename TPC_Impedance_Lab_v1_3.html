Purpose Smartphone-assisted impedance-response teaching laboratory
Method Audio sine source + microphone lock-in detection + loopback calibration
Plots Bode magnitude, Bode phase, Nyquist
Output calibration
The app gain is dimensionless. Enter a measured full-scale RMS output only if you want an estimated excitation voltage shown in the Sweep tab. Impedance calculation itself uses the calibrated voltage ratio.
Hardware connection
OUTPUT
Use a CTIA TRRS breakout or a USB-C audio interface. Headphone/audio output drives Rₛ in series with Zₓ.
MIC
Sense the junction voltage across Zₓ through a protective AC-coupled attenuator into the microphone/line input.
CAL
Before a sample sweep, connect the same sensing input to the audio output through the same protection network and run CALIBRATE LOOPBACK.
SAMPLE
Reconnect the sensing input to the Rₛ–Zₓ junction without changing phone volume, adapter, drive level, or wiring gains.
Measurement assumptions
A single microphone channel cannot directly observe Vₛ and Vₓ simultaneously. This app therefore uses a sequential complex loopback calibration. It works best when audio-path latency, gain and routing remain stable between calibration and sample sweeps.
Phase method: v1.3 starts a fresh sine oscillator at every frequency point and performs DC correction over the complete capture window (not block-by-block). Loopback calibration removes the fixed audio-path gain and phase delay.
Safety: passive low-voltage samples only. Never connect the phone to mains, powered circuits, batteries under test, or unknown external voltages. Protect the microphone input from headphone-level signals with an appropriate attenuator/coupling network.
