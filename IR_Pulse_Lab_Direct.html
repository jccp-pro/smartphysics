📖 How to use

  1. Point the IR remote toward the solar cell connected to your loudspeaker or suitable amplifier.
  2. Place the smartphone's microphone near the loudspeaker; keep background noise low.
  3. Tap START RECORDING and allow microphone access.
  4. Press one remote-control button, then tap STOP (or let the timer finish).
  5. Tap FOCUS BURST to inspect the strongest detected event. Zoom and pan to read pulse timing in milliseconds.
  6. Select MODE 2 · PLAYBACK and IMPORT ORIGINAL AAC / AUDIO FILE to load your proven WhatsApp / music-player audio. Choose Original imported file — DIRECT (default), 1× speed. The browser plays the complete, unaltered file without the app converting it into mono WAV. Use Analyzed mono WAV to compare with the old playback path. The playhead follows the graph.
  7. Go to MODE 3 · CLEAN, choose low- and high-frequency filters, and optionally enable the noise gate. Tap APPLY CLEANING. Use the Signal menu to compare original and cleaned intensity plots and select the cleaned track in Playback to listen.
  8. Export the CSV (intensity proxy vs time), PNG (graph), or WAV (recorded sound).

🔬 Important physics

Many consumer IR remotes transmit bursts of infrared light modulated by a carrier around 38 kHz. The command information is contained in the timing of the bursts and gaps.

A typical phone microphone and audio pipeline are not designed to capture the 38 kHz carrier. A solar cell connected directly to a speaker may also fail to turn that carrier into an audible, faithfully timed signal. If the chart is flat or distorted, that does not establish that the remote sent no IR pulses.

Audio cleanup is approximate: it may change onset times, suppress weak bursts, or distort the envelope. Preserve and compare the unfiltered recording. Neither cleaning nor playback regenerates the original carrier.

For reliable measurements, use an IR receiver / photodiode with a suitable amplifier and envelope detector to create an audio-frequency signal, or a suitable digital interface. Phone microphones can apply filters or processing even when the app requests those features be disabled. Microphone captures still play as 16-bit mono WAV. For imported audio files, DIRECT playback keeps the original file and channels, whereas the comparison and cleaned tracks are converted to mono WAV. Browser-native playback is not guaranteed to use WhatsApp's or YouTube Music's codec, volume, and output route. Neither path guarantees reproduction of the original infrared carrier.

The intensity proxy is RMS² of the microphone signal, in arbitrary units. It is neither calibrated optical power nor a direct measurement of infrared intensity. For better pulse measurements, use the shortest practical analysis time bin and inspect the RMS and peak traces.

🔐 Compatibility & privacy

Use Safari on iPhone or Chrome on Android. Microphone permission normally requires an HTTPS webpage (or a secure local context). An HTML file opened directly from a file manager may work on some devices but not others.

Audio stays on your device and is not transmitted to any server by this app. Imported original-file bytes are preserved for direct playback and unchanged-file download. Recording and export require browser permission and support.
